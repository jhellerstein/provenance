\section{Algebraic Details}
\label{app:algebra}

This appendix develops the structure \emph{within} a single determination and
verifies that support propagation and the filtration interact correctly with
query evaluation.

\subsection{Within a Determination}
\label{app:within}

A determination of depth $k$ is a sequence of conditional commutative monoids
connected by the layer-sequencing operator $\seq$:
\[
  (M_1, \circ_1) \seq (M_2, \circ_2) \seq \cdots \seq (M_k, \circ_k)
  \seq (K, +, \cdot).
\]
Each $M_\ell$ is the commutative monoid of layer-$\ell$ commitments (within a
layer, commitments commute); across layers they do not, since a later layer
depends on which outcomes earlier layers excluded. The operator $\seq$
discharges one layer and conditions the specification for the next; it is not
an algebraic operation. The terminal $K$ is the derivational semiring of Green
et al.~\cite{green2007provenance}, which requires a fixed instance; before
resolution the observation function maps several admissible outcomes to
different instances, and after resolution exactly one remains. Classical
provenance is the degenerate case $k = 0$.

\subsection{Support Propagation under Positive Relational Algebra}
\label{app:propagation}

\begin{proposition}[Support propagation]
  \label{prop:propagation}
  Assume $K$ is positive (Proposition~\ref{prop:support-hom}). For observables
  over a fixed instance selected by a determination,
  \begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
    \item $\supp(P(t_1 \bowtie t_2)) = \supp(P(t_1)) \cap \supp(P(t_2))$;
    \item $\supp(P(t_1) \cup P(t_2)) = \supp(P(t_1)) \cup \supp(P(t_2))$;
    \item $\supp(P(\pi(t))) \supseteq \supp(P(t))$ and
          $\supp(P(\sigma(t))) \subseteq \supp(P(t))$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  (a)~A join is nonzero at $D$ iff both inputs are, as $K$ has no zero
  divisors. (b)~A union is nonzero at $D$ iff some summand is, by
  zerosumfreeness. (c)~Projection adds derivation paths (a union over
  witnesses), so can only enlarge support; selection removes a tuple or keeps
  its whole support.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Combined with Proposition~\ref{prop:filtration} (each $\mathcal{F}_k$ is closed
under $\cup$ and $\cap$), this gives Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration-respected}:
positive-RA composition keeps a support within the maximum level of its
inputs.
