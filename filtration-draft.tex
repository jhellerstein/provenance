\subsection{Filtration of the Determination Semiring}
\label{sec:filtration}

The commitment tower and the determination semiring are not independent
structures: the tower induces a natural \emph{filtration} on the semiring
that connects query-relative depth to algebraic structure.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Fix a specification $\Spec$ with a $d$-layer tower
$L_1 \seq L_2 \seq \cdots \seq L_d$ and let $\mathcal{I}$ be the set of
minimal resolving determinations.
Two determinations $I, I' \in \mathcal{I}$ \emph{agree at level $k$} if
they apply the same commitments in layers $1, \ldots, k$:
\[
  I|_{\le k} = I'|_{\le k}
  \quad\text{iff}\quad
  L_1(I) = L_1(I'),\; \ldots,\; L_k(I) = L_k(I').
\]
Agreement at level $k$ is an equivalence relation on $\mathcal{I}$;
write $[I]_k$ for the equivalence class of $I$ at level $k$.
These equivalence classes refine as $k$ increases:
$[I]_d = \{I\}$ (full agreement = identity) and
$[I]_0 = \mathcal{I}$ (vacuous agreement = everything).

\begin{definition}[Level-$k$ support]
  A set $S \subseteq \mathcal{I}$ is a \emph{level-$k$ support} if it is a
  union of level-$k$ equivalence classes:
  $S = \bigcup_{j} [I_j]_k$ for some $I_j \in \mathcal{I}$.
  Equivalently, $S$ is closed under level-$k$ agreement:
  if $I \in S$ and $I'|_{\le k} = I|_{\le k}$, then $I' \in S$.
\end{definition}

\noindent
Intuitively, a level-$k$ support depends only on the first $k$ layers of
the tower: it cannot distinguish between determinations that agree on
layers $1, \ldots, k$ but differ on layers $k+1, \ldots, d$.

\begin{definition}[Filtration]
  Define $\mathcal{F}_k \triangleq \{S \subseteq \mathcal{I} \mid
  S \text{ is a level-}k\text{ support}\}$.
  This yields a filtration of the powerset:
  \[
    \mathcal{F}_0 \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{F}_1 \;\subseteq\; \cdots
    \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{F}_d \;=\; 2^{\mathcal{I}}.
  \]
  Here $\mathcal{F}_0 = \{\emptyset, \mathcal{I}\}$ (only the trivial
  supports: everything or nothing) and $\mathcal{F}_d = 2^{\mathcal{I}}$
  (all subsets, since level-$d$ classes are singletons).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Filtration respects the semiring]
  \label{prop:filtration-semiring}
  Each $\mathcal{F}_k$ is closed under $\cup$ and $\cap$:
  if $S, S' \in \mathcal{F}_k$, then $S \cup S' \in \mathcal{F}_k$ and
  $S \cap S' \in \mathcal{F}_k$.
  Hence $(\mathcal{F}_k, \cup, \cap, \emptyset, \mathcal{I})$ is a
  sub-semiring of the determination semiring for each $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Unions and intersections of unions-of-equivalence-classes are again
  unions-of-equivalence-classes (equivalence classes partition
  $\mathcal{I}$, so closure under Boolean operations is immediate).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Query-relative depth]
  \label{def:query-depth}
  For a tuple $t$ with determination provenance $\Prov_Q(t)$, the
  \emph{query-relative depth} of $t$ is the minimum $k$ such that
  $\mathrm{supp}(\Prov_Q(t)) \in \mathcal{F}_k$:
  \[
    \mathrm{qdepth}(t) \;\triangleq\;
    \min\{k \mid \mathrm{supp}(\Prov_Q(t)) \in \mathcal{F}_k\}.
  \]
\end{definition}

\noindent
This captures precisely how much of the tower a tuple's provenance depends on:

\begin{proposition}[Characterization of query-relative depth]
  \label{prop:qdepth-characterization}
  \begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
    \item $\mathrm{qdepth}(t) = 0$ iff $t$ is robust
          ($\mathrm{supp} = \mathcal{I}$) or impossible
          ($\mathrm{supp} = \emptyset$).
    \item $\mathrm{qdepth}(t) = k$ iff $t$'s presence or absence depends on
          the first $k$ layers of the tower but is indifferent to layers
          $k+1, \ldots, d$: all determinations agreeing on the first $k$
          layers agree on whether $t$ holds.
    \item $\mathrm{qdepth}(t) = d$ iff $t$'s support is not a union of any
          non-trivial equivalence classes: its presence depends on the full
          determination.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  (a) $\mathcal{F}_0 = \{\emptyset, \mathcal{I}\}$ by definition.
  (b) $\mathrm{supp}(\Prov_Q(t)) \in \mathcal{F}_k$ means the support is
  a union of level-$k$ classes, i.e., closed under level-$k$ agreement.
  If $I \in \mathrm{supp}$ and $I'$ agrees with $I$ on layers $1, \ldots, k$,
  then $I' \in \mathrm{supp}$---so layers $k+1, \ldots, d$ are irrelevant.
  Minimality of $k$ means some level-$(k{-}1)$ class is split by the support.
  (c) Immediate from (b) with $k = d$ (level-$d$ classes are singletons).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Propagation respects the filtration]
  \label{cor:propagation-filtration}
  Under positive relational algebra:
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Join: $\mathrm{qdepth}(t_1 \bowtie t_2) \le
          \max(\mathrm{qdepth}(t_1), \mathrm{qdepth}(t_2))$.
    \item Union: $\mathrm{qdepth}(t_1 \cup t_2) \le
          \max(\mathrm{qdepth}(t_1), \mathrm{qdepth}(t_2))$.
  \end{itemize}
  Query evaluation cannot increase query-relative depth beyond the maximum
  depth of its inputs.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Join computes $\mathrm{supp}(t_1) \cap \mathrm{supp}(t_2)$;
  union computes $\mathrm{supp}(t_1) \cup \mathrm{supp}(t_2)$.
  By Proposition~\ref{prop:filtration-semiring}, both remain in
  $\mathcal{F}_{\max(k_1, k_2)}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The filtration says that the tower is not merely a preprocessing step
before the semiring---it is \emph{reflected inside} the semiring as a
graded sub-semiring structure.
The grading measures how much of the tower a tuple's provenance depends on.
Robust tuples live at grade~$0$ (classical provenance, no tower needed);
fully contingent tuples live at grade~$d$ (the full tower must be
discharged to explain them).
Query evaluation respects the grading: composing tuples through RA+ cannot
push results deeper into the tower than their inputs already were.

\paragraph{Connection to log parsimony.}
A system that only needs to answer queries about tuples at depth $\le k$
need only retain the first $k$ layers of the determination.
The filtration provides the formal basis: $\mathcal{F}_k$ is the
sub-semiring of supports expressible from $k$ layers, and any query whose
outputs lie in $\mathcal{F}_k$ is fully determined by those layers.
This refines the layer-certificate framework
(Appendix~\ref{app:certificates}) with a precise algebraic characterization
of what ``sufficient information'' means.
