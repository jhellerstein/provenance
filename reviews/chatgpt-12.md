The main remaining issue: cross-specification comparison needs a formal bridge

You now say:

different specifications over the same commitment basis share a single filtration.

This is central to semantic-change analysis and SER/SI comparison. But earlier you define:

\[
\mathcal D_{\Spec,\Phi}
\]

as depending on the specification. That creates a slight tension: if SER and SI have different admissible/resolving determinations, what exactly is the common carrier set on which both filtrations live?

This is fixable with a short definition.

Add something like this near Observation 3.3 or before Quantitative Relaxation Assessment:

For cross-specification comparison, we fix an ambient determination
space $\mathcal D^\star_\Phi$ for the workload and commitment basis:
the resolved-history classes generated by the basis before imposing a
particular specification's admissibility predicate.  Each specification
$I$ selects a subset of admissible determinations
$\mathcal D_I \subseteq \mathcal D^\star_\Phi$ and a support function
over that ambient space, mapping inadmissible determinations to $0$.
Thus specifications over the same basis share the same filtration, even
when they differ in which determinations they admit or how they classify
outcomes.

That would make the SER/SI comparison and work-regret/semantic-shift definitions much more formally grounded.

Without this, a reviewer may object: “qdepth under SER and SI are defined over different \mathcal D’s, so how are you comparing them?”

Transaction outcome model: RC depth 0 needs one more alignment

The transaction section defines outcomes as decision traces, ordered by trace-prefix extension and preserving conflict orderings. Then Theorem 4.1 says RC has depth 0 because no cycle type is forbidden.

Conceptually, I agree with your intended claim: RC needs no semantic commitment to satisfy isolation validity. But if outcomes are decision traces that remember conflicting order, then different schedules under RC may still be incomparable as traces. That makes “depth 0” formally delicate.

You can fix this in one of two ways.

The cleanest fix is to say the transactional outcome order is isolation-level relative:

For a given isolation level $L$, the outcome order quotients or
compares decisions that $L$ does not distinguish.  Under RC, conflict
orderings are not semantically observable for isolation validity, so
they are $\Ord_{\mathrm{RC}}$-compatible; under SER or SI, the orderings
needed to rule out forbidden cycles remain observable.

Alternatively, keep one ambient outcome space but phrase Theorem 4.1 as:

depth 0 relative to the isolation-validity abstraction.

That is less elegant but safe.

Right now, “decision traces” plus “RC depth 0” can look inconsistent unless \(\Ord\) explicitly collapses the orderings that RC does not care about.

Datalog theorem is still slightly too general in the body

The prose says “for the representative class below,” which is good. But Theorem 5.1 itself says:

For a finite Datalog{}^\neg program having k stratification layers and d negative SCCs…

That reads as fully general. The appendix goes even further with:

For any finite Datalog{}^\neg program, the canonical layered choice basis is sound and complete…

This is the one place where the paper still invites a logic-programming reviewer to push back. Stable models may not exist; WFS is not always just “the number of consecutive forced choice layers”; and componentwise local stable extensions need careful splitting assumptions.

I would weaken the theorem statement directly:

For the representative class of finite Datalog$^\neg$ programs whose
negative SCCs admit a layered choice basis sound and complete for
stable models, with $k$ stratification layers and choice-depth $d$, ...

Then in the appendix, replace “For any finite Datalog{}^\neg program” with:

For programs satisfying the layered-choice assumptions above...

You can still say this includes the running example and the independent/nested SCC patterns you discuss. But the generality should be conditional.

The transaction lower bound is much improved, but the body proof still hides the explicit witness

The appendix now gives the explicit two-object rw/rw cycle:

T_\infty \xrightarrow{\mathsf{rw}} T_i,\quad
T_i \xrightarrow{\mathsf{rw}} T_\infty.

Good.

But the body proof still says “e.g., by holding a conflicting operation on a hot object indefinitely.” That sounds like the old one-object sketch, which was less precise. I would change the body proof to echo the appendix:

Lower bound: use a long-running transaction $T_\infty$ and, in each
round, a fresh transaction $T_i$ that forms a two-edge Adya cycle with
$T_\infty$ over two objects: $T_\infty \xrightarrow{\mathsf{rw}} T_i$
and $T_i \xrightarrow{\mathsf{rw}} T_\infty$.  The system may abort
$T_i$, preserving $T_\infty$ for the next round, or abort
$T_\infty$, eliminating all future cycles.

That removes any ambiguity and makes the theorem feel more proven in the body.

The “any isolation level” claim should be qualified once more

The theorem says:

Under any isolation level L that forbids some cycle type … with scheduling discretion: worst-case depth \Theta(n).

You add a caveat immediately after the proof:

The lower bound assumes the forbidden cycle pattern is repeatably generable…

That helps. I would move this into the theorem statement itself or make it part (c)’s first sentence.

Suggested replacement:

Under any such $L$ whose forbidden pattern can be generated repeatedly
around a surviving transaction, and with scheduling discretion
(batching, commit-order choice, or victim selection): worst-case
depth $\Theta(n)$.

This avoids an overbroad “any isolation level” nit.

The theorem “Classical provenance is pointwise” is safe, but the proof is too short

The statement is now reasonable. But the proof only handles the “if two determinations disagree, one annotation cannot explain both” direction. The theorem says “iff all resolving determinations produce the same tuple membership and derivational annotation.”

Add one sentence for the other direction:

Conversely, if all resolving determinations agree on tuple membership
and annotation, the common $K$-relation is sound and complete for every
resolution, so a single classical provenance relation suffices.

Also, the phrase “provenance must be indexed by determination” should be softened to:

any sound and complete representation must distinguish those determinations.

That avoids implying every possible representation must literally have type \mathcal D \to K.

One potentially important issue: qdepth under projection/positive RA

The main body states the propagation theorem for join and union only, which is fine. But the prose says “positive relational algebra respects the filtration.” Positive RA also includes projection and selection.

This is probably true, but since the theorem only lists join and union, a picky reader may ask. You can either:

1. add projection/selection to the corollary, or
2. say “join and union, and similarly selection/projection under the standard provenance semantics.”

Since the appendix already discusses projection/selection support behavior, I would add a clause:

Selection preserves support; projection unions supports over preimages,
so both remain in the same filtration level.

That would fully justify the “positive RA” phrase.
